My Story

My journey actually started with philosophy.

Most of you receiving this page know me personally.

And many of you know that I have been on a journey of questioning, exploring, and trying to understand ourselves and this strange and beautiful experience we call life for a very long time.

As most of you know, I started my undergraduate studies at Boğaziçi University as a Philosophy major before transferring to Business Administration.

You may also have heard me say many times that beginning my university journey as a philosophy student was one of the finest things that ever happened to me.

Something happened to my young mind during those years.

A door opened that has never closed since.

It was the door to questioning what I thought I knew. Questioning the things I had accepted as fact. Questioning my assumptions, beliefs, perceptions, and eventually questioning new knowledge as I encountered it.

That curiosity has stayed with me throughout my life.

I have meditated for more than thirty years and explored different practices, philosophies, traditions, and ways of understanding ourselves and our relationship with life.

But more recently, I discovered something that affected me deeply:

Somatic Breathwork.

I had experienced profound states through meditation and other deeper ceremonial practices before. What surprised me about Somatic Breathwork was discovering how much could become accessible through something so fundamental and already within us:

Our own breath.

No substance.
Just breathing.

The experience made me curious enough that I wanted to understand what was happening, learn the modality properly, and experience it more deeply.

That curiosity eventually led me to train and become a Soma+IQ™ Somatic Breathwork Practitioner.

And now I want to share the experience with you.

Why I’m Inviting You

Why I’m Inviting You

I’m not asking you to believe anything.
I’m not trying to convince you of anything.

I simply want you to experience it.

One of the things that fascinated me about this practice was experiencing how consciously working with our own breath can influence how we feel and how we experience our nervous system.

And that brought me back to something I’ve increasingly come to appreciate:

The Soma

The mind is not the only place we carry our lives.

We deal with pressure every day.

Work. Family. Relationships. Responsibilities. Money. Expectations. Uncertainty. Things that happened yesterday, and things we’re already worrying about tomorrow.

These experiences affect the way we think and feel.

But they also affect the Soma, the body.

Stress can show up in our nervous system, our breathing, muscular tension, posture, and the physical sensations we carry with us.

Sometimes we move through that pressure naturally.
Sometimes we don’t.

We hold it in. We tighten. We suppress what we feel. We continue functioning while the pressure continues to build.

And this is where Somatic Breathwork became particularly interesting to me.

Somatic Breathwork

Sometimes We Can Let the Soma Speak

Somatic Breathwork uses conscious connected breathing together with movement, sound, and emotional expression to work directly with the Soma and the nervous system.

And an important part of this practice may initially sound a little counterintuitive:

We don’t begin by trying to calm everything down.

During the active part of the journey, the breathing intentionally becomes more active.

We increase activation and allow ourselves to meet what arises in the body rather than immediately suppressing it, controlling it, or trying to think our way through it.

For me, this is one of the most interesting and powerful aspects of the practice.

We spend so much of our everyday lives holding things together.

Pressure builds.
We tighten.
We control our reactions.

We hold back the sound.
The movement.
The emotion.
The frustration.
The sadness.
The anger.
Or whatever else may be present.

And then we keep going.

Somatic Breathwork gives us a space where, for a while, we can stop holding everything in.

Through the breath, we intentionally increase activation and give the Soma permission to respond.

That response can be different for everyone. It may involve:

Movement.
Sound.
Emotion.
Physical sensations.
Tension.
Tears.
Laughter.
Or something completely different.

There is no particular response we are trying to manufacture.

Nothing needs to be forced.

This is where allowing becomes important. Rather than deciding what should happen, we allow ourselves to experience what is actually present.

We allow the breath.
We allow the sensation.
We allow the movement.
We allow the sound.
We allow the emotion.

And if something genuinely wants to move, we give it space to move.

This is the release.

We don’t always need to ask:

Why am I feeling this?
Where did this come from?
What does this mean?

For a while, we can stop trying to understand everything intellectually.

We breathe.
We allow.
We express.
We let things move.

Then the journey changes.

The active breathing slows.
The body becomes still.
We rest.
We observe.
We allow the system to settle.
And we integrate.

The experience can then create space for us to explore different ways of responding and different patterns in how we relate to ourselves and our lives.

This is the journey:

Breathe. Allow. Release. Repattern. Integrate.

And perhaps one of the things that continues to fascinate me most is how fundamental the tool is.

Our own breath.

The Experience

Come Experience It With Me

This is why I’m sending you this page.

I would love for some of my friends to experience the modality that has become an important part of my own journey.

I’ll guide you through the full Soma+IQ™ The Journey, an approximately 60-minute Somatic Breathwork experience using conscious connected breathing, music, movement, sound, and emotional expression.

We’ll also take some time before the journey to arrive, prepare, and understand what we’re about to experience, and some time afterward to slow things down, come back together, share, and integrate.

Our total time together will be approximately 90 minutes.

And I want you to understand that this isn’t simply 90 minutes of lying down and trying to relax.

It is a journey.

We’ll begin by arriving together and preparing the body and breath.

Then we’ll move into conscious connected breathing.

As the journey builds, the breathing becomes more active and we intentionally increase activation.

We use the breath together with music, movement, sound, and emotional expression to create space for somatic release.

You may feel an invitation to move.
To make sound.
To express something.
To let something out.
Or simply to breathe and experience whatever is happening within you.

Nothing needs to be forced.

This is not about manufacturing a particular response. It is about allowing.

Allowing the experience.
Allowing the Soma to respond.

Allowing whatever genuinely arises to be experienced without immediately trying to suppress it, control it, or explain it.

There is no particular emotional or physical response you are supposed to have.

Your experience is your own.

After the active portion of the journey, we transition into a quieter phase.

The breathing slows.
The body becomes still.
We rest.
We allow the system to settle.
We give the experience space to integrate.

And afterward, we’ll have some time together to talk, reflect, or simply share what we experienced.

There is nothing you need to achieve.
There is nothing you need to perform.
And there is absolutely nothing you need to believe.

Your experience may be very different from someone else’s, and that’s perfectly fine.

My invitation is:

Breathe. Allow. Release. Repattern. Integrate.

And throughout it all:

Experience. Observe. Stay curious.

The Voice

A voice speaks throughout the day.

Sometimes it makes a decision. Sometimes it makes a promise. Then another voice begins to speak.

One voice says, ‘I’m going to bed early tonight.’thenAnother voice still wants to be on the phone at midnight.
The voice that says, ‘I’ll start eating better on Monday.’thenThe voice that makes a completely different choice by Monday afternoon.
The voice that wants to meditate.thenThe voice that says, ‘Skip it today.’
The voice that wants to quit smoking.thenThe voice that lights the cigarette.
The voice that says, ‘I’m not drinking tonight.’thenThe voice that changes its mind when evening comes.
The Self that wants to remain calm.thenThe Self that becomes angry because of a single sentence.
The confident Self.thenThe Self that begins doubting itself after one phone call.

None of these are here to be judged as good or bad.

Simply notice whether any of them feel familiar.

So while all of this is happening, who is at the helm?

Don’t try to answer yet.

Notice.

Self-Mapping

Noticing the different Selves

As you begin to notice these recurring voices and the different Selves that come with them, you may see that each arrives with its own thoughts, emotions, bodily signals, desires, and ways of responding.

You can begin to separate them from one another and observe them.

This is where Self-Mapping begins.

The Body

It is not all happening in your mind.

Noticing the voices is only the beginning.

Because what is happening is not happening only in the mind.

We often talk about how extraordinary the human brain and mind are.

But throughout our lives, we carry another extraordinary system with us that we often overlook: our body.

For a robot or an AI-powered device to perceive its surroundings, we develop cameras, microphones, temperature and motion sensors, and many other technologies.

The human body, meanwhile, is continuously receiving information from both its surroundings and from within itself:

warmth · cold · trembling · sweating · tension · relaxation · changes in breath · changes in heartbeat · pain · fatigue · vitality

In a way, the body is like an extraordinary haptic feedback suit that we carry with us through this three-dimensional world.

It is constantly sending signals.

But how much have we learned to listen to it?

We have become very good at restricting the body and ignoring its signals. We have almost completely forgotten how to listen to it and regulate it.

When the body’s voice is quieted

Modern life often narrows the space in which the body can move and express itself naturally.

We sit in chairs.
We look at screens.
We spend our days in cars, meetings, and within learned ways of sitting and behaving.

When a song arises within us, we rarely hum it.
When music begins, we rarely allow the body to move on its own.
We hold back a sigh.
When the body wants to stretch, we do not stretch.

Soma

At @ Bliss, we call the lived experience of the body from within the Soma. We are not looking at the body merely as something seen from the outside, but as something we feel and experience from within.

Stress and the Body

Stress does not remain only in the mind.

Long-term stress does not only affect how we think and feel. It can also place a real burden on the body.

It can affect our sleep, energy, digestion, breathing patterns, and muscular tension.

When we ignore the signals of the body for a long time, the issue can become more than simply ‘feeling stressed.’

Learned responses

You may have heard of Pavlov’s famous experiments. When a sound that initially carried no particular meaning was repeatedly paired with food, over time the sound alone began to trigger a response.

Human beings are far more complex. But we also learn through repeated experiences.

Thoughts, emotions, and bodily responses can begin to arise together over time.

Sometimes the mind may understand what happened. But the body may still be saying something else.

Regulate

Regulation is broader than any single practice.

At @ Bliss, regulation does not simply mean breathwork.

Sleep, nutrition, nature, movement, rest, meditation, yoga, Tai Chi, prayer, breathing practices, and other practices that support you can all be part of this space.

Somatic Breathwork is one of the powerful and focused practices within this broader field.

Creating space and allowing

Sometimes the point is not to force something to change.

It is to create space for what is arising in the body, feel it without suppressing it, and allow it to move.

It may be a sigh.
It may be a movement.
An emotion, a sound, sometimes tears.

Without forcing.
Without judging.
Without immediately trying to understand why.

Notice. Feel. Create space. Allow.

This is part of what we mean by release.

Four Movements

Regulate → Release → Repattern → Integrate

These four movements do not belong only to breathwork. They can appear through different practices and throughout daily life along the Path.

01

Regulate

Create the conditions that can help the system move toward greater balance. Sleep, movement, nature, meditation, nutrition, breath, and rest can all be part of this.

02

Release

We do not have to hold everything. Create space for what arises. Feel it without immediately suppressing it, forcing it, or trying to solve it in the mind. Allow it to move.

03

Repattern

The old learned response does not have to remain the only response available to you. When a little space opens between what happens and your automatic reaction, another response can become possible.

04

Integrate

Do not leave the new response as a momentary experience. Bring it into daily life. Allow a new choice or response to become more accessible over time.

Daily Life

A practice can create space for you. But the Path continues when the practice ends.

@ Bliss is not a single session, a single technique, or a single method.

The Path continues in how you sleep, how you eat, how you move, and how you rest. It continues in your relationships, your meditation, your yoga, your prayer, Tai Chi, nature, and the choices you make. It also continues as you begin to recognize earlier and earlier when a recurring Self has taken the helm.

This is not a checklist.

The Path is lived.

Why do practices matter?

We do not meditate simply to become someone who meditates.
We do not practice yoga simply to practice yoga.
We do not practice breathwork simply to practice breathwork.

These practices can help us create more awareness, regulation, and space within daily life.

Who is at the helm?

Perhaps the point is not to eliminate all stress from your life.

Perhaps it is not to never become angry, never feel afraid, or always remain motivated.

Life will continue to happen.

But you can begin to notice what is happening a little earlier, listen to your body a little more closely, and create a little more space between an automatic reaction and the response you choose to make.

And perhaps you can take the helm a little more often.

This page has focused mostly on the beginning of the Path. We began with noticing and regulating.

The Path continues. Become Coherent, Resonate, Enjoy, and Remember are waiting further along the Path and will continue to unfold here over time.

And throughout the Path, the same question remains with us:

Who is at the helm?

“I know that there is a voice inside me that tells me things, and that this voice is not my Essence.

I see that, at times, and perhaps much of the time, this voice works like an autopilot that influences or directs the way I live my life.

I can see that my past experiences, habits, and conditioning shape this voice, and with it, the direction the autopilot follows.

And I sincerely want to take the helm again.”

Self-Mapping

Are you ready to take a closer look at one of the voices within you?

We are not a single “I.”

We have inner voices, emotional states, and patterns of behavior that emerge in different situations.

The Fearful I.
The Angry I.
The Doubting I.
The Controlling I.
The Critical I.
The Victim I.
The Achiever I.
And many more.

Self-Mapping is the first practice of choosing one of these inner “I”s and beginning to observe it without judgment.

The goal is not to get rid of it.

First, we learn to see it.

Because an “I” that remains unnoticed can act automatically.

An “I” that is noticed begins to enter Consciousness.

Who is taking control?

Think of a recent moment when you later found yourself saying:

“I wish I hadn't said that.”
“Why did I react like that again?”
“Why did I do that again?”
“Why did I let that affect me so much?”

Now look at the “I” that appeared in that moment.

Can you give it a name?

The Angry I.
The Jealous I.
The Doubting I.
The Controlling I.
The Fearful I.

The name is entirely yours.

There is no correct answer.

We are not asking you to figure out your entire inner world in this exercise.

We are simply going to begin with one “I.”

Three Signals

Before an Unwanted I takes control, it will often leave signals.

We will look for them in three places:

Thought

What went through your mind in that moment?

What did you say to yourself, to someone else, or about what was happening?

Emotion

What did you feel?

  • Anger?
  • Fear?
  • Shame?
  • Anxiety?
  • Jealousy?
  • Inadequacy?

Soma

What happened within your Soma?

  • Did your breathing change?
  • Did your jaw tighten?
  • Did your shoulders rise?
  • Did you feel tension in your chest or stomach?
  • Did your face become warm?
  • Did your posture change?

As we learn to recognize these signals earlier, we increase the possibility of recognizing an Unwanted I before it fully takes control.

Now we are going to make one small but important shift.

Up to this point, we have spoken about this inner state as an “I.”

But once you have given it a name, we begin to create a little distance.

Instead of:“I am angry.”try:“The Angry I is appearing.”
Instead of:“I am afraid.”try:“The Fearful I is appearing.”

Because the “I” you have just mapped is not all of you.

It is a state that arises within you.

And the one who can observe it is still there.

You have completed your first Self‑Mapping.

But the practice does not end here.

In fact, this is where it begins.

In a few moments, we will send you a short guide explaining how to begin working with the “I” you have mapped in everyday life.

For now, we ask you to do just one thing:

The next time this “I” appears, do not try to change it.

Simply notice how early you can recognize it.

Thought.
Emotion.
Soma.

Which one appears first?

This time, you may not notice it until after it has already taken control.

That is okay.

Next time, you may see it a little earlier.

The goal is not perfection.

The goal is to notice earlier.

The moment you notice, you are no longer completely on autopilot.

You are not a prisoner of what you think and feel. With every breath, every moment of awareness, and every choice, you have the capacity to reshape your inner state.

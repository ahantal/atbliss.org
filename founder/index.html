Founder

Ali C. Hantal
Founder, @ Bliss Foundation

A journey from technology to inner transformation, from open standards to the idea of Unity.

Serial entrepreneur. Technology expert. Seeker of Truth. Former national skier. Certified Somatic Breathwork Practitioner and Lifeguard.

But none of these tells the whole story.

Behind Ali's journey is a curiosity that has stayed with him since his university years:

How much of what we think we know to be true is actually true?

The Beginning of Questioning

Ali was born in Arnavutköy, on the shores of the Bosphorus in Istanbul, when it was still a quiet neighborhood.

After high school, he entered the Philosophy Department at Boğaziçi University.

He still remembers his first philosophy class with extraordinary clarity. His professor, the late Arda Denkel, walked into the classroom and began introducing René Descartes and his philosophy of doubt.

That class unsettled many of the dogmatic and memorized certainties he had been taught until then.

He began questioning what he accepted as true, as well as the ideas he would encounter from that day forward.

He later transferred to Business Administration, completed his MBA at the University of Baltimore, and entered the world of entrepreneurship.

Building the Outer World

Ali's professional journey has evolved from the internet to XR technologies, from geographic information systems to spatial computing and geospatial technologies, and today to the convergence of artificial intelligence with the physical world.

He founded XR Masters. Through MyGeoVerse, he began working on geospatial experiences that bridge the physical and digital worlds, as well as AI agents that can be accurately positioned in the real world.

But his relationship with technology was never shaped solely by the question, “What can we build?”

Other questions followed:

Will these technologies be able to work with one another?
Will the digital world of the future be open and inclusive, or will it consist of disconnected, closed systems that fail to respect people's privacy and fundamental rights?
How much agency will people have over the technologies they use?

These questions led Ali toward interoperability, open standards, and protocols.

Today, alongside his work as Founder and CEO of XR Masters, he serves as Co-President of the Open AR Cloud Association, a member of the DEİK/DTİK North America Executive Committee, Adoption Officer of the GeoPose Standards Working Group within the Open Geospatial Consortium, and Leader of the Forum Labs Initiative at the Metaverse Standards Forum.

These efforts converge around a shared purpose:

Contributing to technological foundations that are open, interoperable, inclusive, and respectful of people's privacy and fundamental rights as the physical and digital worlds become increasingly intertwined.

Who Is Technology For?

Over time, another question became increasingly important:

As technology advances, will people and communities become more empowered along with it?

This was one of the questions that led Ali to become a co-founder of the Prosper Foundation.

Prosper focuses on community revival and sovereign technology. It explores a future in which people and communities can develop their own technological capabilities and resilience rather than leaving their future entirely in the hands of a few centralized technology platforms.

Beneath all of this, a common idea gradually became clearer:

Technology should empower people rather than control them.

But that leads to a much more personal question:

Having greater agency over technology matters.

But how much agency do we have over ourselves?

Turning Inward

In fact, this question was not new.

Ali's inner exploration began long before his journey in technology.

From his early university years, he began reading the Qur'an, the Bible, the Torah, the Zohar, the Bhagavad Gita, the Masnavi, the Tao Te Ching, and other ancient and sacred texts.

He went on to explore Shamanism, Sufism, theology, Hermetic philosophy, Taoism, Tantra, Kabbalah, and Christian mysticism.

Across traditions that emerged in different geographies, different eras, and different languages, he repeatedly encountered traces of a similar Truth:

We are One.

The material world we experience through separation and duality is not the whole story.

At the heart of different traditions, he also repeatedly encountered an ancient principle that became intertwined with this understanding:

Do not do to another what you would not want done to you.
Do not harm what does not harm you.
Do not destroy what does not threaten your life.

For Ali, this is not simply a moral command imposed from outside, but a natural consequence of realizing Unity.

As we become capable of recognizing in another the same life we recognize within ourselves, compassion can cease to be a rule we are expected to follow and begin to emerge as a natural expression of Unity.

The Sixth Sense

Since childhood, Ali had wondered why schools taught only five senses.

After years of meditation and inner exploration, he began to see something with increasing clarity:

A person who reaches a truly silent mind may become capable of knowing through the heart.

Over time, the spiritual journey began to shift from a search for Truth somewhere outside himself toward a process of remembering what was already within.

He explored what might exist beneath personality, past experiences, and the mind's endless narration.

Behind the sense of separation, he began to see Unity, and behind Unity, the same Source from which everything arises.

Different traditions have used different names for this lived Truth.

Some called it Light. Others called it Allah, God, Chi, or the Force.

The words changed.

But the direction they pointed toward became increasingly familiar:

Living in alignment with what is more sacred than oneself.

Living with the Flow

Ali's understanding of the Path does not rest on a single teaching.

It draws from the surrender of Sufism, Shamanism's relationship with nature, the flow of Taoism, Kabbalistic understandings of existence, and the teachings of love found in Christian mysticism.

The Taoist concept of wu wei holds a special place in this understanding.

Wu wei does not mean doing nothing. It points toward acting without applying unnecessary force against the natural flow of life.

Like a surfer who does not try to create the wave, does not struggle against it, but instead feels it, aligns with it, and moves with the power it carries.

Ali's understanding of the Path is similar:

Rather than forcing life, learning to listen to it, becoming aware of the Universe, and learning to move consciously with its flow.

Mind, Heart, and Soma

Ali has practiced meditation for more than thirty years and continues the daily practices of what he calls the @ Bliss Path.

Encountering Somatic Breathwork opened a new door in his journey.

Alongside many things he had understood intellectually and known by heart, he began to discover that the body is not merely a physical structure we carry. Its internally experienced state, in a reciprocal relationship with the mind, affecting it and being affected by it, the Soma, is also part of this journey.

He began observing more closely how stress, thoughts, and accumulated emotions that have not been able to flow can reveal themselves through breath, posture, tension, and bodily sensations.

At the same time, he began to notice how what happens within the Soma can also influence our mental and emotional state.

Breath became a two-way bridge between mind and Soma.

The journey was no longer only about understanding intellectually and knowing by heart.

It was also about listening to the Soma and becoming aligned somatically.

Why @ Bliss?

Ali believes human civilization is facing an existential risk on a scale unprecedented in known history.

Developments in artificial intelligence are rapidly increasing our technological power. At the same time, he sees the uncontrolled development of AI, without sufficient questioning and without alignment with the common good of humanity, as a significant risk in itself.

Wars, global authoritarianism, and the gradual destruction of our planet's ecosystems, in his view, also converge at this same turning point.

All of this brings Ali back, again and again, to the same questions:

As our technological Capacity grows, is our Consciousness developing with it?
As we create increasingly powerful tools in the outer world, how aware are we of our own inner world?
Who or what directs our Attention?
What governs our fears, reactions, and choices?
And within all this noise, can we still hear our Essence?

@ Bliss Foundation was not created to provide ready-made answers to these questions.

It emerged to create a space where we can explore them together.

Somatic Breathwork is one of the tools along this Path.

But @ Bliss is not only about breathwork.

It is part of a larger journey of remembering.

May We Be With the Force

There is a phrase Ali has always loved:

“May the Force be with you.”

But over the years, he began to wonder whether perhaps it should be said a little differently:

“May we be with the Force.”

Because the Force was always here.

We are the ones who moved away from it.

Perhaps the Path is not about finding something that is not out there, but remembering how to align again with something we have always been part of.

Today

Today, Ali facilitates individual and group Somatic Breathwork sessions and holds space for people exploring their own path back to Self.

He divides much of his time between St. Thomas, U.S. Virgin Islands, Türkiye, and Washington, D.C.

And the curiosity that began in his university years still remains at the center of his journey:

What is it that we do not know or have forgotten that could bring us a little closer to the One, to Love, and to Truth?

And as he always says:

Live Long and Prosper!

You are not a prisoner of what you think and feel. With every breath, every moment of awareness, and every choice, you have the capacity to reshape your inner state.
